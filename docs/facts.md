# Verified facts ("Did you know?")

Every fact shown in the app was checked against a NASA page or a stable scientific source
before publication (rule 13 of the project brief). Facts that could not be verified were
removed. Last full check: 15–16 November 2026.

## Ocean currents (NASA SVS 5479, ECCO-2)

| Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| The Gulf Stream can flow at up to about 9 km per hour (2.5 m/s) at the surface. | NOAA Ocean Service, "How fast is the Gulf Stream?", https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/gulfstreamspeed.html | **Corrected**: the brief said "one of the fastest currents on Earth"; that claim was only found in secondary sources and was dropped |
| Agulhas rings, giant swirls of warm water, can last more than 2 years. | Satellite-altimetry studies: average crossing of the South Atlantic about 3.5 years; one ring tracked as a coherent body for about 2 years (Froyland et al. 2015, "Studying an Agulhas ring's long-term pathway and decay with finite-time coherent sets") | Verified (scientific literature) |
| Ocean currents carry heat around the planet. | SVS 5479 (the Gulf Stream carries heat to northern latitudes), https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5479 | Verified |
| These swirls come from a model that uses spacecraft and buoy observations. | SVS 5479 ("observations from spacecraft, buoys, and other in situ measurements") | Verified |
| The Humboldt Current flows along the west coast of South America carrying cold water. | NASA Earth Observatory, coastal Peru (2015) | Verified |
| ~~The Kuroshio current off Japan carries water of about 20 to 25 °C.~~ | No NASA or NOAA source found | **Removed** |

## Aerosols (NASA SVS 5669, GEOS)

All five facts come from the description of https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5669 and were verified there:
aerosols travel thousands of miles; Saharan dust crosses the Atlantic; strong ocean winds lift
sea salt (Hurricane Erin, the first Atlantic hurricane of 2025, is visible); Kilauea and Etna
release sulfates; seasonal burning in southern Africa sent smoke over the Atlantic.
